Sending the same packet over two wireless links can improve the probability of delivery before a deadline, but each replicated packet consumes an additional transmission opportunity. The value of that opportunity depends on whether the second link can deliver packets that the first link fails to deliver. Shared obstacles can cause both links to fail together, reducing this benefit even when their individual failure probabilities remain unchanged. The resulting design question is when two-link replication improves deadline reliability enough to justify its transmission cost under a specified resource budget.

Existing reliability analyses provide useful starting points for this question. The analysis in \cite{independent_replication} considers independent link failures, making it possible to assess replication when failures on one link carry no information about failures on the other. When shared causes make failures co-occur, an independence assumption may yield an optimistic assessment of the replication gain. The work in \cite{correlated_reliability} characterizes correlated failures for a fixed replication configuration. It therefore addresses the effect of dependence on reliability, while leaving selection under a per-packet transmission budget outside its scope. These two studies motivate examining reliability characterization and resource-constrained selection together in a common model.

This paper studies one-shot transmission of a packet within a common deadline. The available actions are single-link transmission and replication over two links; retransmission protocols and queueing dynamics are outside the model. Deadline success means that at least one transmitted copy is delivered within that deadline. We assume that the marginal link failure probabilities and the joint probability of both links failing are known. Using these quantities, we choose between the available actions to maximize deadline success probability subject to a constraint on the expected number of transmissions per packet. Randomized selection is allowed, so the resource constraint governs the expected transmission cost rather than requiring an identical action for every packet. This formulation makes both the additional transmission cost and the dependence between failures explicit.

The paper makes the following three contributions:

1. We derive the deadline success probability for the supplied two-link joint failure model. The characterization uses the known marginal and joint failure probabilities to evaluate single-link transmission and two-link replication under the same deadline.

2. We obtain an optimal selection rule within this probabilistic model and the stated single-link and two-link action set. The rule allows randomized selection and maximizes deadline success probability subject to the expected transmission budget, identifying how that budget constrains the feasible use of replication.

3. We evaluate the rule numerically for three values of the joint failure parameter. The results illustrate that, with marginal failure probabilities fixed, the marginal reliability benefit of replication decreases as co-occurring failures become more probable. Together with the budget constraint, this dependence determines the value of spending an additional transmission opportunity within the model.
