**Abstract**

We study finite-blocklength achievable rates for finite-alphabet discrete memoryless channels under a fixed input distribution. We establish a nonasymptotic achievability lower bound for blocklengths \(n\geq n_0\) and average error probability constraints \(0<\epsilon<1/2\). The bound follows from a refined random-coding analysis that retains a term relaxed away by conventional estimates. For parameters in a specified set \(\mathcal S\), the resulting lower bound is tighter than the existing bounds considered in the paper; no improvement is guaranteed outside this set. The analysis thus identifies a source of slack in the finite-blocklength achievability argument and shows that preserving the corresponding term strengthens the achievable-rate guarantee within \(\mathcal S\).

**Contributions**

- We establish a nonasymptotic lower bound on the finite-blocklength achievable rate of finite-alphabet discrete memoryless channels under a fixed input distribution, valid for \(n\geq n_0\) and an average error probability constraint \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).
- We refine the random-coding analysis by retaining a term that conventional estimates relax away, exposing the analytical source of the improvement. The detailed proof is provided in the appendix.
- We establish a tighter achievable-rate lower bound than the existing bounds considered in the paper within the specified parameter set \(\mathcal S\). This comparison is restricted to \(\mathcal S\), with no improvement guaranteed outside it.
