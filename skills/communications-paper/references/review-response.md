# Review and revision responses

Distinguish self-review, simulated pre-submission review, and a response to an actual decision. A simulated review is feedback, not a prediction or an editorial verdict. Use supplied reviewer comments for actual replies; never manufacture missing comments.

## Review the supplied evidence

Identify manuscript version, target, supplied sections and unavailable material. Read beyond the abstract when evaluating technical claims. For a partial draft, state the coverage and mark unresolved checks as not assessable; absence from an excerpt does not prove absence from the full paper.

Assess the actual contribution and its support before language. Useful issue classes include:

- Question and positioning: unclear gap, unsupported novelty, missing comparison with a specifically relevant work.
- Model and information: assumptions hidden or inconsistent, future or oracle information used by a claimed practical method.
- Mathematics: a precise proof gap, unsupported quantifier, misidentified guarantee, asymptotic result generalized to finite regimes.
- Evaluation: unequal resources, irrelevant comparator, insufficient support for a specific claim, mismatch between metric and conclusion.
- Presentation: unclear definition, inconsistent notation, broken logical link or duplicated evidence.

Do not produce a quota of criticisms or demand fashionable baselines unrelated to the question. A strong analysis paper need not introduce an algorithm. An idealized model can be valuable when its purpose and limits are explicit.

For a substantive concern, provide:

`issue → exact location or quoted identifier → why it matters → evidence/status → smallest adequate resolution`

Severity follows impact. A demonstrated central error differs from an unverified concern; an editorial “minor revision” label does not prove every request is minor. If unable to inspect a derivation or experiment, say what would resolve the uncertainty. Do not describe an unperformed check as a discovered error.

Default deliverable: brief account of the contribution and strengths, prioritized substantive issues, then optional minor edits. End with concrete next steps only when useful. Avoid fabricated numerical acceptance probabilities or claims to speak for the journal.

If independent reviews are requested and tools permit them, obtain genuinely separate assessments before synthesis. Do not claim independent reviewers when one model merely writes different personas. There is no mandatory reviewer count.

## Prepare a point-by-point response

Read the decision and all supplied comments. Retain identifiers and separate multipart requests. Do not merge away an inconvenient question. Choose a response for each part: correct, clarify, provide evidence, narrow the claim, perform additional work, or respectfully disagree with a technical reason.

For a long revision, maintain a working table:

| Comment | Concern | Action | Work status | Evidence/change location | Remaining gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Original identifier | What must be resolved | Specific response | Proposed / author-reported complete / verified complete | Actual section, equation, figure or excerpt | Open item or none |

Use the table internally or show it if requested; a short reply does not need a tracking system.

**Status controls tense.** “We have added” requires the change to exist or a clearly identified author report. Do not turn proposed experiments into completed work. Author-reported completion may support a draft response, but the handoff must say it has not been independently verified. A plan alone requires future/conditional wording or a labeled draft placeholder, not a ready-to-submit claim of completion.

A useful reply makes four points in natural prose: acknowledge the specific concern; answer it directly; explain the evidence or change; point to its actual location. Thank the reviewer briefly without excessive flattery. Keep disagreement focused on assumptions, evidence or scope, and offer a clarification or bounded claim when that resolves the misunderstanding.

If a requested result is unavailable, complete the defensible reply and identify exactly what remains. Do not promise future work on the authors' behalf without authorization. If it is unnecessary to the paper's bounded claim, explain why; disagreement is allowed when technically justified.

## Coordinate manuscript and letter

When manuscript revision is authorized, make the relevant change rather than only drafting a promise. Then verify that quoted replacement text, theorem/figure numbers, section labels and page/line pointers agree with the actual revised version.

- Never invent page or line numbers. Use stable section/equation labels while pagination is unsettled; mark exact pagination for later verification.
- Clearly distinguish verbatim reviewer/manuscript quotations from paraphrases.
- Recheck dependent claims in abstract, Introduction and conclusion after a substantive correction.
- Recheck all replies affected by changed numbers, terminology or pagination. Do not repeat a full audit for an unrelated comma fix.
- Follow the editor's or user's requested marked-copy format; red text, italics or a particular table are not universal IEEE rules.

Return the usable replies or revision with an honest readiness statement: what is complete, what is author-reported, and what still depends on evidence. A polished letter cannot make an unresolved research issue complete.
